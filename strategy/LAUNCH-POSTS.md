# Launch posts — Show HN + Reddit (copy-paste ready)

Both live or die on the same rule: **HN/Reddit destroy marketing, reward honest builder-talk.** Your edge is
real — solo UK dev, built a real thing that reads HN itself. Lean all the way into that. No "revolutionize,"
no "game-changer." Write like a tired dev who's proud of a thing and wants honest feedback.

The #1 predictor of success on both: **you sitting in the thread for 4-6 hours replying to every comment,
fast and humble.** That engagement is what pushes it up. Do not get defensive. A found bug = "thanks, fixing."

## SHOW HN

**When:** Tue–Thu, ~8–10am US Eastern (1–3pm UK). Not weekends.
**Submit as:** a link to `https://threadverse.ai` with the title below, THEN immediately post the first comment.

### Title (must start with "Show HN:")
```
Show HN: Threadverse – an AI reads ~5,000 posts/day so I only see the 50 worth it
```
*(alt if you want the mechanism up front: `Show HN: A feed reader that filters by meaning, not keywords`)*

### Your first comment (post this yourself, right after submitting)
```
I got tired of scrolling HN, X, Bluesky, Mastodon and YouTube separately, and
tired of muting 200 keywords and still drowning in noise. So I built Threadverse.

You describe the feed you want in plain English — e.g. "serious AI research and
model releases, no hype threads, no drama" — and it reads ~5,000 posts a day
across those platforms and keeps the ~50 that actually match. The filtering is by
meaning, not keyword matching, so it catches the stuff a blocklist never would.

Two things I care about that most readers don't do:
- The rejected pile stays visible. You can see the ~4,950 it threw out and rescue
  anything it got wrong — I didn't want a black box deciding what you don't see.
- No engagement algorithm, no ads. It's a tool you configure, not a feed that
  optimizes for your attention.

It's solo-built, in the UK. Free tier covers HN, Bluesky and Mastodon (3 feeds,
10 refreshes/day). Pro adds X + YouTube and unlimited refreshes.

Honestly still-rough bits I'd love feedback on: [FILL IN 1-2 REAL ONES before
posting — e.g. "cold-start feed quality on niche topics", "mobile layout",
"latency on the first build of a big feed"]. Tear it apart — that's why I'm here.
```
**Then:** reply to EVERY comment for the next 4-6h. Technical, humble, fast. "Good catch, that's a real
limitation" beats any defense. If someone says a competitor does X, agree where true and say what's different.

## REDDIT

**Warm the account FIRST** (non-negotiable): genuinely comment on a few r/rss threads over 1–2 days before
posting your own — help someone pick a reader, answer a question. A 0-history account posting its own tool
gets auto-removed or banned. Read each sub's sidebar rules (some require flair, some have a self-promo thread).

### r/rss  (the bullseye — small but pure intent)
**Title:**
```
I built a feed reader that filters by meaning instead of keywords — would love feedback from RSS folks
```
**Body:**
```
Long-time feed-reader user, first time building one. My frustration was that
keyword muting never actually works — you block 200 words and the noise just
rephrases itself.

So I made Threadverse: you describe the feed in plain English ("deep systems
posts, no career drama, no hot takes") and an AI reads ~5,000 posts/day across
HN, Bluesky, Mastodon (+ X and YouTube on the paid tier) and keeps the ~50 that
match by meaning. The part I'm proudest of: it shows you the rejected pile so you
can audit and rescue what it got wrong — no black box.

No algorithm optimizing your attention, no ads. Free to start (HN + Bluesky +
Mastodon). Genuinely want feedback from people who take feeds seriously — what
would make this replace your current reader, and where does the "filter by
meaning" idea break down for you?
```

### r/SideProject  (maker-friendly — solo-builder story lands)
**Title:**
```
Solo-built an AI feed reader that reads 5,000 posts/day and keeps the 50 worth it
```

### r/InternetIsBeautiful  (can spike big — but strict; read rules)
Only post here if it fits their "genuinely useful/interesting site" bar. Title describes the *thing*, not the
brand: `A site where you describe your ideal feed in plain English and an AI filters 5,000 posts down to 50`.
No self-promo framing at all here — it has to read as "look at this cool thing," which it genuinely is.

## After either lands
- Put `threadverse.ai` in every bio + a pinned reply, but NEVER lead with "sign up."
- Every commenter who engages = a warm contact. Reply, and note the sharp ones — some become users/advocates.
- Screenshot good feedback; it's fuel for the next post and for the "solo dev built X" story that later
  reframes you for the platforms that don't work yet (TikTok/IG).
- If a real bug/criticism comes up repeatedly, fix it fast and reply "done" in-thread — that visible
  responsiveness is worth more than the original post.
